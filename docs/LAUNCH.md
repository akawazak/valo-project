# VantaVault launch pack

This is a small, human-posted launch plan. Do not automate posts, mass-message users, or post where self-promotion is not allowed.

## What to link

- Homepage: `https://vanta-vault-app.vercel.app/`
- Store checker: `https://vanta-vault-app.vercel.app/valorant-store-checker/`
- Loadout manager: `https://vanta-vault-app.vercel.app/valorant-loadout-manager/`
- Match history: `https://vanta-vault-app.vercel.app/valorant-match-history/`
- Portable Windows download: `https://github.com/akawazak/valo-project/releases/latest/download/VantaVault-portable.exe`

Use the feature page that matches the conversation. A store-checker post should not send people to a generic homepage if the store-checker page answers their question first.

## Launch post: community forum or Reddit

**Title:** I built an open-source Windows companion for checking your VALORANT store, loadouts, and match history

**Body:**

I made VantaVault because I wanted my store, cosmetic presets, profile progression, and locally saved match history in one desktop app instead of across different screens.

It is free and open source. Store information requires the local Riot Client to be running; it does not predict offers or unlock cosmetics. Account sessions and saved match data stay on the device.

Project page: https://vanta-vault-app.vercel.app/

Source code / releases: https://github.com/akawazak/valo-project

I would genuinely appreciate feedback on the loadout workflow and which screen would be most useful next.

## Short-form video outline (20–30 seconds)

1. Start with the daily-store screen: “I got tired of opening multiple screens just to check my VALORANT stuff.”
2. Show a saved cosmetic preset: “So I made VantaVault: store offers, loadouts, and presets in one Windows app.”
3. Show profile or match history: “It also keeps your account view and locally saved match history together.”
4. End on the download page: “It’s free and open source—link in bio. It needs the local Riot Client for store information.”

## Before every post

1. Check the community’s promotion rules and disclose that you built the app.
2. Use a real screenshot or short screen recording; a plain link performs poorly.
3. Lead with one outcome relevant to that community (store, presets, or progression), not a long feature list.
4. Stay to answer questions for at least a few hours. Early replies matter more than posting repeatedly.
5. Record the post URL and date. In Vercel Analytics, compare page views and the `Download` custom event afterward.

## What success looks like

Start with a small test: three relevant community posts and one short video, spread across a week. Keep the channel or post only when it produces meaningful visits or download clicks. Change the hook or screenshot before posting again; do not simply repeat the same message.
